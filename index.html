On the Council

Record

01

No property tax increase

We have not raised property taxes since I joined the Council.

02

A $16 million 911 and emergency operations center

We funded a $16 million Emergency Operations Center and 911 dispatch building on the city campus without a bond or a tax increase.

03

Fire Station 54

We opened Fire Station 54 on the east side, staffed it with new firefighters, and cut response times in that part of the city.

04

North Davis dispatch

We now run unified 911 dispatch for the north end of Davis County from our Emergency Communications Center.

05

Secondary water

We brought Kays Creek’s pressurized secondary water system into the City, and a project to connect our reservoirs and pipe water to the west side is underway.

06

2700 West

We built the 2700 West connection to the West Davis Corridor, with a $3 million match from Davis County.

07

Parks and trails

We opened Harmony Park on the west side, added pickleball courts at Ellison and Andy Adams Parks, and kept expanding our trail system.

How I serve

Governing Principles

Layton is the largest city in Davis County and I work to ensure it remains the best place to live, work, recreate, and raise a family. Municipal government is the government closest to the people it represents. Consequently, it must be people-focused and data-driven. I strive to make the best decisions for your family and mine. It is an honor to represent you on the Layton City Council.

Issues

Where I stand

01

Fiscal Conservatism

Unlike the federal government, a municipal government like Layton cannot print its own money. Layton’s budget must be centered in reality—just as you and I run our own household budgets. I will seek to improve necessary city services while remaining vigilant in defending the sacred nature of taxpayer funds so that they are never wasted. We must only spend what we bring in.

02

Public Safety

Government’s first duty is to protect its people. And the day-to-day security of our community begins at the local level. I vow to ensure that Layton City is always at the forefront of public safety by fully funding, training, and supporting our police officers in ways that keep our families protected, our men and women in uniform safe, and bad actors off the streets.

03

Water

For far too long our water policy has been detached from the realities of our dry, semi-arid desert climate. I will continue to steer our policy conversations and ordinances toward commonplace conservation to assure that our kids and grandkids have the water that they need to thrive.

04

Good Governance

Layton City exists to deliver necessary services to the residents it serves. I work to assure the City’s water, sewer, streets, police, fire, parks, and other utilities and services are adequately funded, efficiently trained and maintained, and ready for citizen use whenever they are called upon.
